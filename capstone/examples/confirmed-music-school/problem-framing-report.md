## Independent Review

A faithful, well-separated frame for a confirmed one. It records the user's situation, effects, seasonal versus year-round timing, the money constraint and the hiring idea at the strength the user gave them, and it keeps the board's view marked as anticipated. Its main limits are that no desired state exists, that there is no measured baseline for the time burden the user weighs most, and that the framing quietly ties the families' problems to the admin load more firmly than the user did.

**Strengths**

- The user's hedges survive. 'The hours, I guess' is kept as a belief. The move from 'it's everything' to naming hours and lost program-planning time is recorded as a refinement, not left as a contradiction.
- The timing distinctions are preserved precisely. Reply delays peak in September and January and ease otherwise, while tuition chasing is monthly, evening-bound and not seasonal.
- The hiring idea is kept apart from the problem, labelled undecided, and left out of the problem statement.
- The board constraint is correctly qualified as anticipated rather than heard, since the user has not raised it yet.
- The unknowns are named honestly, including that the user has never tracked admin time and that the cause of missed schedule changes is not established.

**Limitations**

- No desired state was stated, so the frame describes only the current condition. The 'Not known' entry acknowledges this. *Why it matters:* Without some picture of better, such as how much evening time back, which tasks leave the user, or what reply times for families would be acceptable, a reader cannot tell what would count as the problem being resolved. Options also cannot be compared against anything.
- The cost the user weighs most, hours, is unmeasured. The user has never tracked admin time, and the split across emails, billing, scheduling and grant reports is unknown. The only quantified elements are the dozen families chased monthly and the three-to-five-day peak replies. *Why it matters:* The central burden rests on felt experience. That is legitimate, but it limits how precisely anyone can size the problem or judge whether a given amount of relief would matter.
- Part B and the problem statement present slow replies and missed schedule changes as costs of the user carrying all the admin. Yet the frame itself says the cause of missed schedule changes is not established. The user listed these effects side by side without explicitly attributing them to their workload. *Why it matters:* The family-side effects may share a cause with the user's time burden, or they may not. A reader could take the link as established and treat these as one problem when they might be two related ones: the user's capacity, and the reliability of communication to families.
- The frame rests entirely on the user's view. Parents' waits and missed schedule changes are reported second-hand, and teachers, who originate schedule changes, are not considered among those affected or involved. *Why it matters:* How often families are actually affected, and where schedule information breaks down, may look different from the perspectives of families and teachers. Confidence in the family-side effects is therefore limited.

**How well the problem statement fits the framing**

The statement reflects the frame well. It is concise and specific, includes the key figures, avoids naming a cause, and keeps the hiring idea out. Three things limit it. It has no desired condition, so it is not measurable against a target. It drops the user's 'I guess' when calling program-planning time the cost they weigh most. And by following 'handles all of its admin alone' with 'Families are also affected,' it implies the family effects stem from the user's workload, which the frame lists as not established.
