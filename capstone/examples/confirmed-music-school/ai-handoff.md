# Problem-Framing State

Written by a problem-framing tool at the end of one session. It stands in for the conversation, which is not included.

## Status
CONFIRMED BY THE USER. The user confirmed: "This accurately represents my current understanding for now, including appropriately represented beliefs, unknowns and unresolved material." Confirming does not mean that anything here is proven or complete, and it does not turn an assumption into a fact or make one of the tool's assumptions yours. It does not mean a solution was chosen.

## How to read this
- Part A is what the user supplied, with its standing. It is the basis.
- Part B is the tool's integrated interpretation of Part A. It is derived, not the user's words.
- Keep beliefs, estimates, unknowns and qualifications as they are. Do not silently resolve ambiguity, turn a belief or estimate into a fact, or present an interpretation as something the user said.
- A proposed solution is an idea the user is considering. It is not a requirement or a decision, and nothing here shows it is right.
- A desired state describes what a better condition would look like, not how it would be brought about.
- Nothing here says what the user wants to do next. Use it as context for whatever they ask.

## Part A. What the user supplied

### Situation
- The user runs a small nonprofit community music school with about 140 students and nine part-time teachers, and teaches twelve hours a week themselves.
- The user does all the admin: parent emails, tuition billing, teacher schedules and grant reports.
- The user hasn't brought the idea to the board yet. (relation: Qualifies the board constraint: its view is anticipated, not yet heard.)

### Effects
- Parents sometimes wait days for a reply.
- Teacher schedule changes sometimes don't reach the families.
- The user feels stretched too thin across all of it, not one thing. [the user's belief]
- The cost that weighs most is the hours, 'I guess': the user's evenings are gone, and they never have time anymore to plan new programs, which is why they started the school. [the user's belief] (relation: Names which part of being stretched thin matters most to the user.)

### When and where it applies
- It's worst in September and January when enrollment picks up; that's also when parents wait longest for replies, sometimes three to five days. (relation: Applies to the peak months.)
- Outside September and January it eases off some and the user can answer parents faster. (relation: Applies to the rest of the year; does not cover tuition chasing.)
- Chasing late tuition happens every month, about a dozen families, always in the evenings, and the user hates doing it. (relation: Year-round, unlike the seasonal peaks.)

### Constraints
- Money has been the main obstacle: the budget is tight, twenty hours a week would use most of the small surplus, and the board is cautious about ongoing costs.

### Desired state
Not stated by the user.

### Proposed solution (the user's idea, not decided)
- Hire a part-time office manager, maybe twenty hours a week.

### What the user said they do not know
- The user has never tracked how much time the admin takes.

## Part B. Current understanding (derived by the tool)

### The problem
The user carries all of the school's administration on top of their teaching. Its costs fall in two places: on the user, whose evenings are consumed and who has no time for program planning, the reason they started the school and the cost they weigh most; and on families, through slow replies and schedule changes that sometimes don't reach them. The load has a seasonal shape for parent communication but a steady monthly evening burden from tuition chasing.

### Who is affected
- The user (runs the school and teaches)
- Parents and families of students

### Patterns and cases (the tool's reading)
- Parent reply delays peak in September and January with enrollment and ease off at other times, while late-tuition chasing recurs every month regardless of season, so the evening burden is not only seasonal.

### Standing of the proposed solution
Hiring a part-time office manager of about twenty hours a week is the user's idea; it has not been brought to the board, and funding it would use most of the small surplus.

### Not known
- What a better situation would look like for the user, such as how much time back or which tasks off their plate would matter most.
- How often schedule changes fail to reach families and why; the cause is not established.
- How the board would respond to an ongoing cost of this kind.

### Problem statement (the draft the user confirmed)
The user, who runs a roughly 140-student nonprofit community music school and teaches twelve hours a week, handles all of its admin alone. Their evenings go to it, including chasing late tuition from about a dozen families every month, and they no longer have time to plan new programs, the cost they weigh most. Families are also affected: parents sometimes wait days for replies, three to five days in the September and January enrollment peaks, and schedule changes sometimes don't reach them.
